# 🏛️ PLINTH — Spatial Curation & Material Exhibition

Plinth is a slow-curated visual social space designed for architects, materials scientists, and sculptors to log aggregates, structural frames, and spatial models. It has been built following strict, non-templated editorial design guidelines to establish an opinionated point of view.

---

## 🎨 Visual System & Identity Tokens

### 1. Palette
- **Chalk Plaster (`#F9F9F8`):** Background surface resembling raw gallery plaster walls.
- **Architectural Charcoal (`#1F2022`):** Core structural text, layout gridlines, and solid block boarders.
- **Pigment Ultramarine (`#0F2FD9`):** High-saturation cobalt accent used for primary interactive states and highlights.
- **Raw Concrete (`#E2E2DF`):** Subtle masonry borders separating the layout columns.
- **Anodized Silver (`#7F8082`):** Secondary metadata text and monospaced coordinates.

### 2. Typography
- **Display Signage:** *Syncopate* (sans-serif) — Wide, uppercase, heavily spaced typeface mimicking clean concrete formwork markings.
- **Exhibition Body:** *Instrument Sans* (sans-serif) — Refined editorial sans-serif face for curatorial bios and composition descriptions.
- **Technical Metrics:** *DM Mono* (monospace) — Spaced technical monospaced text representing stress tests, porosity, and coordinate grids.

---

## 🏛️ Grounding the Subject

- **Concrete Subject:** A materials exhibition and curation repository.
- **Audience:** Spatial designers, masonry curators, and architects.
- **Single Job:** Exhibit structural specimens, cataloging their physical parameters alongside an interactive 3D spatial plinth viewport.
- **Design Signature:** An interactive 3D platform rendering modular concrete, glass, metal, and clay pillars that rotate dynamically in real-time as the cursor moves across the viewport.

---

## ⚙️ Running the Exhibit

Since the interface is built with vanilla HTML5, CSS 3D transforms, and JS:
1. Open the [index.html](file:///c:/Users/HP/Desktop/Inventory%20System/plinth/index.html) file directly in your web browser.
2. Move your mouse over the **Plinth Viewport** to shift the isometric perspective in 3D space.
3. Select specimen cards at the bottom (e.g. *Concrete Column Cast*, *Fluted Borosilicate Pane*) to view their composition metrics.
4. Input details into the **Catalog Log** form to curate a new material specimen and automatically stack its corresponding 3D blocks.
